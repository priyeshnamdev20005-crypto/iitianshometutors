How it works

How a week runs

The same routine for every student, from Class 8 to a JEE repeater. This is how a week actually runs.

  1. Day 0 — Free demo classA real class on a real chapter, at your home. You decide after seeing it.
  2. Class day — Concept before problemOne-to-one. No memorised steps — if the student cannot explain the why, we stay on it.
  3. Same evening — DPP goes homeA practice sheet on exactly what we just did, done the same day.
  4. Any day — Doubts on WhatsAppSend a photo of the question, get the working back the same day.
  5. Every Sunday — Test, marked by meA full paper on the week’s work, marked strictly, returned with mistakes marked.
  6. Sunday night — Update to parentsA short written message: what was covered, what the test showed, what needs work.

Start early

JEE & NEET Foundation, and Olympiads

For students who want to start JEE or NEET preparation in Class 8 or 9. The school syllabus stays first; on top of it, the habit of competitive-level thinking is built slowly — so Class 11 never feels like a jump.

Class 8 · 9 · 10

JEE Foundation

Physics, Chemistry and Maths taken to one level above the school textbook — NCERT first, then foundation-level problems. Concept building, MCQ accuracy, negative-marking discipline and speed, so Class 11 does not feel like a wall.

School marks stay up. The base gets built underneath.

Class 8 · 9 · 10

NEET Foundation — Physics & Chemistry

The Physics and Chemistry half of NEET foundation, taught the same way: NCERT line-by-line, then application questions. Numericals, mole concept, units and measurement — exactly where NEET aspirants lose marks later.

Biology needs a separate teacher — I do not take it.
