Why one-to-one works for JEE

JEE is not a syllabus problem, it is a practice problem. Almost every student who is struggling knows the theory well enough to explain it back to you. What they cannot do is look at an unfamiliar question and decide, in twenty seconds, which idea to use. That skill only comes from solving problems while someone watches how you think and corrects the approach, not just the answer.

In a batch of forty, nobody watches how your child thinks. In a one-to-one class I can see the exact moment a student goes down the wrong path on a rotational-motion problem, stop there, and fix the habit. That is the whole reason I teach this way.

What the paper actually asks

JEE Main has three subjects with equal weight — Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics — 75 questions and 300 marks, with negative marking on the multiple-choice section. Two attempts a year, and the better score counts. JEE Advanced is a different animal: fewer questions, multi-concept, multiple-correct options, and partial marking that punishes guessing.

So the preparation is two-layered. Main rewards speed and accuracy on standard patterns. Advanced rewards depth on fewer ideas. We build the Main-level command first, chapter by chapter, and then go deeper on the chapters that repay depth.

How I take each subject

Where most marks are lost

Physics

Started from the derivation, not the formula list. A student who can derive the result knows when it does not apply — and that is exactly what Advanced tests.

  • Mechanics until it is automatic: kinematics, NLM, work-energy, rotation
  • Electrodynamics and modern physics for Main scoring
  • Every numerical done with units and a sanity check on the answer
The easiest marks to gain

Chemistry

Three subjects wearing one name, so they get three different treatments.

  • Physical — pure numericals, treated like Physics
  • Organic — mechanisms and reagents, never rote reactions
  • Inorganic — the one place where planned revision beats understanding, so we schedule it
Where the time goes

Mathematics

Long papers are won on Maths and lost on Maths. The goal is fewer steps per question, not more.

  • Calculus and coordinate geometry as the backbone
  • Algebra: quadratics, complex numbers, sequences, binomial
  • Shortcut-spotting drilled on previous-year questions
The weekly rhythm

How a JEE week runs

Nothing here is unusual. It is just done every single week, which is the part that is actually hard.

  1. Concept classWe take one chapter at a time, from the derivation up to Advanced-level questions. Your child works problems during the class, not after it — I need to see the thinking.
  2. DPP the same eveningA Daily Practice Problem sheet goes out on the day of the class, while the chapter is still warm. Six to ten questions, graded easy to JEE-Advanced level.
  3. Doubts on WhatsAppBetween classes, a photo of the question gets a written solution back. A doubt that waits four days becomes a chapter your child quietly avoids.
  4. Test every SundayA timed paper on what was covered, marked by me with the mistakes written out — not just a score. Negative marking included, because the exam has it.
  5. Previous-year questionsChapter-wise JEE PYQ sets with step-by-step solutions, so your child sees how the same idea has actually been asked over the years.
  6. Parent updateA short, honest message each week: what was covered, the test score, and what is genuinely not working yet.

Class 11, Class 12 or a dropper year

Class 11 is where JEE is actually won or lost, and almost nobody believes that at the time. Mechanics, physical chemistry and algebra from Class 11 come back in the paper and in every Class 12 chapter that builds on them. A student who is shaky in Class 11 spends Class 12 repairing instead of moving forward.

Class 12 is a schedule problem more than a teaching problem. Boards and JEE both need feeding, and they reward slightly different answers. We keep both papers in view and use the board syllabus as the base, adding JEE depth on top rather than running two separate preparations.

Droppers need an honest diagnosis first. One year is enough time only if we know exactly which chapters are weak, so we start with a test, not with Chapter 1. Then it is volume — solved with a plan, not with panic.

Questions parents ask

JEE tuition — what parents ask me

Do you teach both JEE Main and Advanced?

Yes. The Main syllabus is the base and every student covers it fully. Advanced-level depth is added on the chapters that repay it, for students who are targeting Advanced. There is no point doing Advanced-level rotation problems with a student who is still shaky on free-body diagrams, so the depth comes when the base is ready.

My child is in a big coaching class already and still not improving. Why?

Usually because nobody is watching how they solve. Lectures deliver the syllabus well; they cannot see that your child always sets up the equation from the wrong reference frame. That is a one-to-one job. Many of my students keep their coaching class and use me to fix the practice side.

Do you teach Biology as well, for students doing both JEE and NEET?

No. I teach Physics, Chemistry and Maths only. If your child is preparing for NEET too, I take the Physics and Chemistry — which is half of the NEET paper — and you keep a separate Biology teacher. I would rather tell you that plainly than take the subject and do it badly.

How many classes a week, and how long is each class?

It depends on the class and how many subjects you want. Most JEE students take three to six classes a week across the three subjects. Call me and tell me the class, the subjects and how much time your child actually has — I will tell you honestly what is workable.

What are your fees?

Fees depend on the class, the number of subjects and how many classes a week we run. All the material — notes, DPP, PYQ banks, Sunday tests — is included in the fees, so the number I quote is the number you pay. Call or WhatsApp on 79774 75848 and I will give you the exact figure for your case.

Is the demo class really free?

Yes, and it is a real class. Tell me the chapter your child is struggling with and we will do that chapter. At the end your child will know whether my teaching suits them, which is the only question worth answering.
