Who actually decides the Class 8 syllabus

Worth knowing before anything else: CISCE does not set an ICSE Class 8 syllabus the way it sets Class 9 and 10. There is no Class 8 board exam and no prescribed chapter list or paper pattern. CISCE publishes broad curriculum guidance for Classes I to VIII; the detail — textbooks, chapters, marks, paper format — is fixed by your child's own school.

In practice almost every ICSE school in Mira Bhayandar uses Selina Concise, and that is what makes a common Class 8 plan possible at all. If your school uses something else, bring the book to the first class and I will teach from it.

The consequence for you as a parent is simple: the Class 8 marksheet means very little, and comparing it across schools means nothing at all. What matters is whether the chapters are actually closed.

What Class 8 should leave behind

The habits, not the marks

By the end of the year these should be automatic. Everything ICSE Class 9 does is built on them.

  • Algebraic manipulation without hesitation — expand, factorise, rearrange
  • Identities used on sight rather than derived every time
  • Indices and exponent laws applied without checking the book
  • Every physics number written with its unit, every time
  • Chemical formulae written from valency instead of memory
  • Balancing equations as a routine, not a puzzle
  • A full solution written out in steps, because ICSE marks the steps
  • Reading a word problem twice before starting to write

Why I push algebra so hard in Class 8

Almost every student I meet who is struggling in ICSE Class 9 Physics is not struggling with physics. They are struggling to rearrange v = u + at to find a, or to handle a fraction inside a fraction. The physics idea was understood in the lesson; the algebra failed in the working.

The same thing happens in Class 9 Chemistry with formulae and in Class 9 Maths with logarithms — a chapter ICSE teaches and CBSE does not teach at all, and one that punishes shaky index work immediately.

So Class 8 tuition with me is mostly quiet, unglamorous work on manipulation and notation, wrapped around the school syllabus rather than instead of it. It does not produce a dramatic marksheet. It produces a Class 9 that feels manageable.

Questions parents ask

ICSE Class 8 tuition — what parents ask me

Is there an official ICSE Class 8 syllabus?

Not in the sense parents mean. CISCE runs no Class 8 examination and prescribes no Class 8 chapter list, textbook or paper pattern — it publishes broad curriculum guidance for Classes I to VIII and leaves the detail to the school. That is why two ICSE schools in the same area can have noticeably different Class 8 papers. Most use Selina Concise, which is what this page is built around.

Class 8 marks do not count. Is tuition a waste of money?

It is a waste if it is used to chase Class 8 marks. It is the best-value year there is if it is used to close gaps — because in Class 9 and 10 every hour goes to the board syllabus and there is no spare time to repair Class 7 algebra. I would rather take a student in Class 8 for a year than in Class 10 in October.

Do you teach Biology?

No. Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics only. I would rather tell you that on the first call than take the subject, teach it out of a guide book and waste your child's year. For Biology you keep a separate teacher, and plenty of my students do exactly that.

My child's school uses a different book, not Selina.

Then we use your child's book. Selina is the common case, not a requirement. Bring whatever the school has prescribed to the first class — the physics and the mathematics do not change with the publisher, and I would rather your child works from the book their teacher sets questions from.

Should we start Olympiad preparation in Class 8?

If your child enjoys the subject, yes — Class 8 is a good year for it precisely because the school syllabus is light. NSTSE, the SOF papers and the MSCE Scholarship Exam for Std 8 are all reasonable targets. If your child is already struggling with the school work, no; fix that first. Details are on the olympiad coaching page.
