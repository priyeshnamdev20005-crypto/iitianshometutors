Class 10 foundation — finishing early

A board year, so the board comes first. But for a student who started in Class 8 or 9, this is the year the Class 12 syllabus gets opened — and the payoff arrives in Class 11, when everything is a second look instead of a first one.

The board year comes first. Then the head start.

Let me be clear about the order, because this is a board year and the board result follows your child into every admission form for the next two years. Class 10 board preparation is the priority. The foundation work runs alongside it and gives way to it whenever the two compete — in January and February there is no foundation work at all.

Within that constraint, a Class 10 student who has been on this plan since Class 8 or 9 has something unusual: the Class 11 content is already familiar, so the school Class 10 syllabus takes far less time than it takes their classmates. That freed-up time is what goes into Class 12 content.

And after the board exam there is a long, famously wasted gap before Class 11 starts. That gap is worth two months of solid work.

What we open in Class 10

Class 12 content, at foundation depth

For students continuing from Class 9. A student starting fresh in Class 10 does a different, shorter plan — see the FAQ.

Start early

Mathematics — calculus

The big one, and the reason to start it early.

  • Limits and continuity
  • Differentiation, from first principles
  • Applications — tangents, maxima and minima
  • Introduction to integration
  • Remaining Class 11 coordinate geometry and binomial theorem

Calculus met at fifteen with no exam pressure is a different subject from calculus met in Class 12 with boards and JEE eight months away.

Patterns, not lists

Chemistry — organic begins

  • General organic chemistry — inductive and resonance effects, stability
  • Nomenclature and isomerism
  • Reaction mechanisms, taught as mechanisms and not as lists
  • Hydrocarbons
  • Electrochemistry and chemical kinetics, beginning

Organic chemistry is where a slow, early start beats a fast one most clearly, because it is a subject of patterns and patterns take time to see.

Applied, not revised

Physics — electricity and beyond

  • Electrostatics
  • Current electricity
  • Magnetic effects and magnetism
  • Rotational motion and gravitation completed from Class 11
  • Beginning of optics

Class 11 mechanics should already be solid from the Class 9 year; this is the year it gets applied rather than revised.

How the Class 10 year is sequenced

Board first, always

The calendar does most of the work here.

  1. June to SeptemberBoard syllabus pushed ahead of the school schedule so it finishes early. Foundation work runs at about a third of the time — mostly calculus and organic chemistry basics.
  2. October to DecemberBoard syllabus completed. Board paper practice begins. Foundation work continues at a reduced pace.
  3. January and FebruaryBoard only. No foundation work at all. Past papers, presentation, speed and revision.
  4. MarchBoard exams. Nothing else exists.
  5. April and MayThe gap most students waste. Two months of concentrated Class 12 content with no school and no exam — the single most productive block in the whole three-year plan.
  6. Into Class 11Your child walks into Class 11 having already met most of what the year will teach. Class 11 becomes practice and depth rather than first exposure.
An honest word about promisesNo teacher can promise your child a rank or a selection, and any teacher who does is selling you something. What an early start genuinely buys is time — the syllabus met twice instead of once, and Class 11 and 12 spent on problems rather than on first exposure. That is a real advantage and it is the one I will put in writing.

What the three years actually buy

By the end of Class 10 a student who has run this plan since Class 8 or 9 has met most of the Class 11 syllabus and opened the Class 12 syllabus in all three subjects. That is not the same as having finished JEE preparation, and I will not dress it up as that.

What it means in practice is that Class 11 and Class 12 change character. Instead of meeting rotational motion, organic chemistry and calculus for the first time under time pressure, your child is meeting them for the second time and spending the hours on problems. That is where the real preparation happens, and most students never reach it because they are still learning content in December of Class 12.

Less stress in Class 11 and 12 is not a marketing phrase here; it is the arithmetic of doing the same work over four years instead of two.

What I will not claim: a rank, a selection, or a result. Those depend on your child's work over four years, on their aptitude, and on a national exam with lakhs of candidates. Anyone who promises you a single-digit rank is telling you something they cannot know. What I can promise is the plan, the material, the honest weekly update, and that I will tell you if it is not working.

Questions parents ask

Class 10 foundation — what parents ask me

My child has done no foundation work before. Is Class 10 too late to start?

Not too late to start, but the plan is different and I will say so honestly. A student starting fresh in Class 10 does the board year properly and uses the April–May gap for a concentrated introduction to Class 11 mechanics, the mole concept and trigonometry — not the Class 12 syllabus. That is still a real advantage going into Class 11. Pretending we can compress three years into one would just cost you money and your child confidence.

Will this hurt the board marks?

It should not, because the board comes first in the sequence and January and February are board-only. In practice students on this plan usually score better in the board exam than they would have, because the syllabus is finished by December and the last two months are pure practice. If I ever see the board work slipping, the foundation work stops until it recovers.

Can you really finish the JEE syllabus by Class 10?

No, and I would not trust anyone who says they can. What is realistic over Class 8 to 10 is meeting most of the Class 11 syllabus and opening the Class 12 syllabus, so that Class 11 and 12 are a second pass rather than a first one. That is a genuine and substantial advantage. “Full JEE syllabus completed in Class 10” is a slogan, not a plan.

Do you guarantee selection or a rank?

No. Nobody can, and a teacher who offers you that is selling something. Selection depends on four years of your child's work, their aptitude, and a national exam with over a million candidates. What I will commit to is the plan, the material, a marked test every week, an honest update every week, and telling you plainly if this is not working for your child.

Do you teach Biology?

No. Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics only. I would rather tell you that on the first call than take the subject, teach it out of a guide book and waste your child's year. For Biology you keep a separate teacher, and plenty of my students do exactly that.
