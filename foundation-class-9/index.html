Class 9 foundation — Class 11 begins

This is the year the plan actually starts. School board work continues as normal, and alongside it we begin the Class 11 Physics, Chemistry and Maths syllabus — properly, from first principles, with no rush and no board exam breathing down the year.

Why Class 9 and not Class 11

Look at what Class 11 actually is for most students. In June they meet mechanics, the mole concept and trigonometric identities for the first time, at speed, in a batch of sixty, while also carrying a school syllabus and the knowledge that JEE is eighteen months away. Everything is new and everything is urgent at the same time. That is why Class 11 breaks so many good students — not because the content is impossible, but because there is no room in the year to be confused for a week.

Class 9 has that room. There is no board exam. The school syllabus is light enough to finish in half the time. So the hardest ideas in Class 11 physics can be met slowly, at thirteen or fourteen, with time to be stuck and time to recover.

Then in Class 11 your child meets those ideas for the second time. That is the entire advantage, and it is a big one.

What we start in Class 9

The Class 11 syllabus, from the beginning

Board work first, always. This is what runs alongside it.

The backbone

Physics — mechanics, properly

The whole Class 11 mechanics block, taught from the derivation rather than the formula.

  • Units, dimensions and measurement
  • Kinematics in one and two dimensions, vectors
  • Newton's laws, friction, constraint relations
  • Work, energy and power
  • Beginning of circular motion and centre of mass

Mechanics is the chapter everything else in JEE physics leans on. Two years is the right amount of time for it.

Mole concept first

Chemistry — the numerical half

  • Mole concept and stoichiometry, until it is boring
  • Atomic structure — Bohr, quantum numbers, orbitals
  • Periodic table and periodicity, reasoned rather than memorised
  • Chemical bonding — VSEPR, hybridisation, shapes
  • States of matter, basic thermodynamics

The mole concept is the single biggest dividing line in school chemistry. Given two years, every student gets it.

Trigonometry early

Mathematics — the toolkit

  • Sets, relations and functions
  • Trigonometry — identities, equations, properties of triangles
  • Quadratic equations and the theory of equations
  • Sequences and series
  • Straight lines and coordinate geometry, beginning

Trigonometry is done early and thoroughly because physics needs it immediately, not because the maths syllabus asks for it.

What this is not

It is not the Class 11 syllabus compressed into Class 9. It is the Class 11 syllabus started in Class 9, at a pace a fourteen-year-old can actually absorb, with the school board work still coming first. By the end of Class 9 a student on this plan has covered a substantial part of Class 11 Physics and Chemistry and the Class 11 maths toolkit — not all of it, and I would be lying if I said otherwise.

It is also not for every child. It needs a student who is comfortable in their school syllabus, who is genuinely interested, and who has four to six hours a week to give to this on top of school. If any of those three is missing, the honest answer is to do the school year well and start in Class 10 or Class 11. I would rather say that on the phone than take the fees.

Which board does this suit?

All of them, with one note. CBSE students have the smoothest run, because NCERT is the line JEE and NEET are written on. ICSE students carry an advantage in numerical work and a disadvantage in a few NCERT-specific topics, which we cover separately. SSC students need a little more English-medium technical vocabulary early on. IGCSE and MYP students usually need the most computation drilling and the least conceptual help.

Questions parents ask

Class 9 foundation — what parents ask me

Will the school marks suffer?

They should improve, not suffer, and that is not a sales line — a Class 9 student learning Class 11 mechanics finds the Class 9 physics chapter easy afterwards. The risk is real if the foundation work is allowed to crowd out the school syllabus, which is why the school work comes first every week and the foundation work pauses if the school report slips.

How many hours a week does this take?

Four to six hours a week of class time across the three subjects, plus homework. For a Class 9 student that is manageable alongside school and still leaves evenings and a weekend. If your child's school already runs long hours or there are other commitments, tell me on the first call and we will plan something smaller or start in Class 10 instead.

Is this for JEE only, or NEET too?

Both, for two of the three subjects. Physics and Chemistry are shared between JEE and NEET, and that is what I teach. A NEET-track student adds Biology with a separate teacher — Physics and Chemistry are 360 of the 720 NEET marks and they are the half most medical aspirants lose marks in. The NEET page explains that side.

Do you teach Biology?

No. Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics only. I would rather tell you that on the first call than take the subject, teach it out of a guide book and waste your child's year. For Biology you keep a separate teacher, and plenty of my students do exactly that.

What if we start and it turns out to be too much?

Then we stop the foundation part and keep the board tuition, and nothing is lost — the Class 11 work done so far still sits in your child's head and helps in Class 11. I would much rather adjust in November than have a student quietly drown until March. You will know before I do, but I will tell you if I see it first.
