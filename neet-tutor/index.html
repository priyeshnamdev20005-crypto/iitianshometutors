The honest part first

Most NEET students are strong in Biology and weak in Physics. Ask any teacher and they will tell you the same thing. Biology rewards careful reading and memory, which medical aspirants are usually good at. Physics rewards setting up a problem correctly, which they are usually not trained in — and by the time it shows up in a mock test score, there is not much time left.

So the marks are sitting in Physics and Chemistry. Out of 720, they are 360. A student who goes from 90 to 160 in Physics and Chemistry has gained more than a student who squeezes ten more marks out of Biology, and the Physics gain is far more achievable.

What I do not teach: Biology. You keep your Biology teacher or coaching class. I take Physics and Chemistry and I take them properly. I would rather tell you that on the first call than take the subject, teach it from a guide book, and waste your child's year.

Where NEET students bleed marks

NEET Physics

Not as deep as JEE, but wider and unforgiving on time. Roughly 45 questions to be done in about 45 minutes with negative marking.

  • Formula-to-question mapping drilled until it is instant
  • Mechanics, current electricity, optics and modern physics get the most hours
  • Every practice set timed, because untimed practice hides the real problem
The most scoring of the three

NEET Chemistry

The most NCERT-faithful subject in the paper. Questions are frequently lifted straight from NCERT lines, so we work from NCERT and not from a thick reference book.

  • Inorganic and organic done NCERT line by NCERT line
  • Physical chemistry numericals kept short and repetitive
  • Exceptions and named reactions put on a revision schedule
The weekly rhythm

How a NEET week runs

  1. Concept class at your homeOne chapter at a time, taught on the NCERT line, with your child solving questions in front of me.
  2. DPP the same eveningA practice sheet the same day, NEET-pattern MCQs with negative marking, while the chapter is fresh.
  3. NCERT back-exercise disciplineEvery NCERT question in the chapter gets done. For NEET Chemistry this is not optional and it is where easy marks live.
  4. Doubts on WhatsAppSend a photo of the question, get a written solution back — not a voice note saying 'we will see it tomorrow'.
  5. Test every SundayTimed, NEET-pattern, marked by me. The mistakes are written out so your child can see the pattern in their own errors.
  6. Previous-year questionsNEET PYQ sets chapter-wise with solutions, so your child recognises how ideas are actually asked.

Class 11, Class 12 and droppers

Class 11 carries a large share of the NEET Physics and Chemistry paper, and students who treat it as a warm-up year pay for it twice. We cover it at full NEET depth as it happens.

Class 12 means the board exam and NEET at once. For Maharashtra students there is often MHT-CET in the picture as well — the same Physics and Chemistry, asked in a slightly different way and with no negative marking. If that is your case, read the MHT-CET page; the preparation overlaps heavily and we plan it as one.

Questions parents ask

NEET tuition — what parents ask me

Why don't you teach Biology?

Because I am a Physics, Chemistry and Maths teacher and Biology is not my subject. A teacher who claims all four is usually teaching at least one of them from a guide book. Your child's Biology deserves someone who lives in that subject.

Then how do we manage Biology?

Most of my NEET families keep a separate Biology teacher or a coaching class, and use me for Physics and Chemistry. It works well — the subjects do not overlap, so there is nothing to coordinate. Several parents have told me it is cheaper than one coaching package too.

Is NEET Physics easier than JEE Physics?

Less deep, but faster. JEE will give your child one brutal multi-concept problem; NEET gives 45 questions to finish in roughly 45 minutes with negative marking. So we train differently: for NEET the drill is recognising the pattern and computing cleanly under time, not extracting the hardest possible question.

My child is in Class 12 and scoring badly in Physics with NEET in a few months. Is it too late?

No, but the plan has to be honest. We test first, find the chapters that carry the most marks and are the weakest, and work in that order rather than starting from Chapter 1. Chasing every chapter equally is what wastes the last few months.

Do you use NCERT or a reference book for Chemistry?

NCERT first, and for NEET almost only NCERT, because that is where the questions come from. Reference material is used for extra practice, not as the base. For Physics we add PYQ banks and my own question banks on top.
