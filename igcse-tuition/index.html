What makes IGCSE different to teach

An Indian board paper rewards a student who has covered the syllabus. A Cambridge paper rewards a student who can apply it to something they have not seen, explain it in clear English, and handle the experimental side in writing. The content is not harder than ICSE — in several places it is lighter — but the question style is genuinely different, and a student moved from an Indian board into IGCSE usually loses marks for a term before adjusting.

The three decisions that actually shape an IGCSE result are made in school, not in the exam hall: Core or Extended, Paper 5 or Paper 6 for the practical, and whether to add 0606. Those are worth thinking about carefully, so they are on this page rather than buried.

The three syllabuses

Topics and papers

Codes and paper structures below are from the current Cambridge syllabus documents.

Current to 2028

Physics 0625

Six topics: Motion, forces and energy; Thermal physics; Waves; Electricity and magnetism; Nuclear physics; Space physics.

Core sits Paper 1 (MCQ, 45 min, 40 marks), Paper 3 (Theory, 1 h 15, 80 marks) and one practical paper.
Extended sits Paper 2 (MCQ, 45 min) and Paper 4 (Theory, 1 h 15, 80 marks) and one practical paper.

Practical: Paper 5 is a real lab test, 1 h 15; Paper 6 is Alternative to Practical, 1 hour. The school chooses.

Current to 2028

Chemistry 0620

Twelve topics: States of matter; Atoms, elements and compounds; Stoichiometry; Electrochemistry; Chemical energetics; Chemical reactions; Acids, bases and salts; The Periodic Table; Metals; Chemistry of the environment; Organic chemistry; Experimental techniques and chemical analysis.

Same paper structure as Physics — Core takes Papers 1 and 3, Extended takes Papers 2 and 4, and everyone takes Paper 5 or Paper 6.

50% non-calculator

Mathematics 0580

Nine topics: Number; Algebra and graphs; Coordinate geometry; Geometry; Mensuration; Trigonometry; Transformations and vectors; Probability; Statistics.

Core: Paper 1 non-calculator (1 h 30, 80 marks) and Paper 3 calculator (1 h 30, 80 marks).
Extended: Paper 2 non-calculator (2 h, 100 marks) and Paper 4 calculator (2 h, 100 marks).

The non-calculator paper is half the grade and is the single most under-practised thing in IGCSE Maths.

A Level bridge

Additional Mathematics 0606

A separate qualification taken alongside 0580 Extended, not instead of it. Single tier, grades A* to E.

Two papers of two hours and 80 marks each; Paper 1 non-calculator, Paper 2 calculator.

Content: functions, quadratics, polynomials, logarithms and exponentials, straight lines, circles, circular measure, trigonometry, permutations and combinations, series, vectors and calculus.

This is where most IGCSE maths tutoring demand actually sits, and it is a real step up.

Questions parents ask

IGCSE tuition — what parents ask me

Which syllabus codes do Indian schools use?

0625 Physics, 0620 Chemistry and 0580 Mathematics — the A*–G graded versions. The 9–1 numbered variants (0972, 0971, 0980) were introduced for the UK grading system and are used in a different Cambridge administrative zone. Check your school's entry codes if you want to be certain; the teaching is the same either way.

Core or Extended — which should we take?

Extended, unless there is a clear reason not to. A Core entry caps the grade at C, which closes doors at A Level and at university application time. Cambridge deliberately overlaps the tiers at C, D and E so a mis-tiered student is not destroyed, but the ceiling is real. If your child is genuinely struggling, the honest answer is to work at it in Grade 9 rather than to drop the tier in Grade 10.

Is Additional Maths 0606 worth taking?

If your child is heading for A Level Maths, engineering or any quantitative degree, yes — 0606 covers calculus, logarithms, series and vectors, and it makes the jump to AS Level far less brutal. It is a real step up from 0580 and it needs its own class time, not leftover time. If your child is not continuing with mathematics, skip it.

Paper 5 or Paper 6 for the practical?

Your school decides, not you. What matters is that Paper 6, Alternative to Practical, is not the easy option — it is a written paper about experiments, testing exactly the same skills, and it demands precise description of method, variables and sources of error. Students assume it is the soft choice and under-prepare. Both are worth 20% of the grade.

Do you teach Biology?

No. Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics only. I would rather tell you that on the first call than take the subject, teach it out of a guide book and waste your child's year. For Biology you keep a separate teacher, and plenty of my students do exactly that.
