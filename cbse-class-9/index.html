What actually changed in 2026-27

NCERT has replaced the Class 9 Science and Class 9 Mathematics textbooks outright. This is not the 2022-style rationalisation where chapters were trimmed — these are new books with new names, new chapter titles and a different order.

Science is now a book called Exploration, thirteen chapters. Maths is Ganita Manjari, published as Part I with eight chapters.

Two consequences parents need to know. First, Gravitation has left Class 9, and so have Matter in Our Surroundings, Improvement in Food Resources, Natural Resources and Why Do We Fall Ill. Second, Probability and progressions have moved down into Class 9 Maths from Class 10.

And one honest caveat: Ganita Manjari is labelled Part I, and its eight chapters do not cover the Geometry, Statistics or Surface Area and Volume content that the CBSE Class 9 syllabus still requires — that is 25 marks of Geometry alone. A Part II is clearly coming but NCERT has not released it yet. Anyone showing you a confident Part II chapter list is guessing.

Class 9 is where the CBSE gap opens

CBSE Class 9 has a reputation for being easy, and the school marksheet usually supports that reputation. Then Class 10 arrives with a national board paper and the reputation collapses.

The reason is that Class 9 in CBSE is taught for understanding and assessed gently, while Class 10 is assessed by an external examiner who has never met your child. Roughly half the Class 10 paper is competency-focused — case-based, data interpretation, application — and those questions are unforgiving of a student who learned the chapter but never practised using it.

What I do in Class 9, therefore, is not extra syllabus. It is the same syllabus taken to the depth Class 10 will demand: every NCERT exercise worked properly, plus questions that force the idea to be used in an unfamiliar setting.

Questions parents ask

CBSE Class 9 tuition — what parents ask me

Are the Class 9 NCERT books really new this year?

Yes. Both Science and Mathematics have new NCERT first editions for 2026-27 — Exploration for Science and Ganita Manjari for Maths. New chapter names, new sequence, some topics moved between classes. Second-hand books from an elder sibling will not work this year, and neither will last year's notes.

Gravitation is not in the new Class 9 Science book. Is that a problem for Class 10?

It is worth knowing rather than worrying about. Gravitation has moved out of the new Class 9 book, and Class 10 Science does not contain it either — Class 10 Science is unchanged and still has thirteen chapters covering chemical reactions, life processes, light, electricity and magnetism. Where it does matter is JEE and NEET foundation work, and in that track I teach gravitation anyway.

Why has the Maths book only got eight chapters?

Because it is Part I. NCERT has released Ganita Manjari Part I with eight chapters, and the CBSE Class 9 syllabus still requires Geometry worth 25 marks, plus Statistics, Linear Equations in Two Variables and Surface Area and Volume, none of which are in Part I. Part II has not been published yet and I will not guess at its contents. Meanwhile we teach those units from the CBSE syllabus and from question practice, which is what your child's school is doing too.

Do you teach Biology?

No. Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics only. I would rather tell you that on the first call than take the subject, teach it out of a guide book and waste your child's year. For Biology you keep a separate teacher, and plenty of my students do exactly that.

Does CBSE Class 9 have a board exam?

No. The Class 9 annual exam is set and conducted by the school. The structure follows the CBSE scheme — 80 marks theory plus 20 internal, three hours — but the paper is the school's. The twice-a-year board exam you may have heard about is Class 10 only.
