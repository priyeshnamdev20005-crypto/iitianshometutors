Part one

Start here — the exams your school already runs

These come through the school, cost a little, and are the right first step for almost every child. None of them will change your child's life on their own. What they do is show a child, early and gently, that there is a world of questions beyond the textbook.

1

Science Olympiad Foundation (SOF)

IMO, NSO, IEO, IGKO and NCO — the olympiads most schools run+
Classes1 to 12 (NCO up to Class 10)
SubjectsIMO Maths · NSO Science · IEO English · IGKO GK · NCO Cyber
Who registersThe school, on the SOF portal. A student can apply directly only if the school is not participating
Forms openRoughly May to September
Exam monthsOctober to December · Level 2 in January–February
FeeAbout ₹125 per subject, plus a small school charge
LevelsTwo — Level 2 from Class 3 onwards
PaperMCQ on an OMR sheet, 35–50 questions, one hour, normally no negative marking
Official sitesofworld.org

Who should take it

  • Wants concept clarity rather than only marks
  • Is heading towards JEE, NEET or the bigger olympiads later
  • Enjoys Maths, Science, English, GK or computers

What the child gets

International Rank 1 gets a cash prize, gold medal and certificate. Zonal rank holders get cash awards, school rank holders get medals, and every child gets a participation certificate. Cash prizes have ranged from about ₹5,000 to ₹50,000 depending on rank.

  • Builds fundamentals early
  • Sharpens logical and analytical thinking
  • Helps later in JEE and NEET
  • Certificates sit well in an academic portfolio
Priyesh Sir’s note

This is the one I would start with. It runs through the school, the fee is small, and the questions are just hard enough to show a child that school marks and real understanding are two different things.

2

SilverZone Foundation

iOM, iOS, iOEL and the other SilverZone olympiads+
Classes1 to 12 (iGK, iSSO, iCOM mostly up to Class 10)
SubjectsiOM Maths · iOS Science · iOEL English, plus GK, Social Studies and Computers
Who registersUsually the school; individual registration is allowed if the school does not take part
Forms openJune–July, closing around September–October
Exam monthsNovember to January · Level 2 in January–February
FeeAbout ₹120 to ₹150 per subject
LevelsTwo, and for some subjects a third Scholar's Test
PaperMCQ on OMR, 35–50 questions, 60 minutes, usually no negative marking
Official sitesilverzone.org

Who should take it

  • Wants a second olympiad alongside SOF
  • Needs more practice at the same level
  • Is in Class 6 to 10 and getting serious about competition

What the child gets

International rank holders receive a cash prize, trophy, medal and certificate; zonal rank holders get medals and certificates; every child gets a participation certificate.

  • Steadier concept practice
  • A competitive habit without heavy pressure
  • A base for the harder olympiads like IOQM and NSEJS
Priyesh Sir’s note

Slightly easier than SOF and perfectly good as a second exam. But be clear about what it is — a foundation-level olympiad, not the national HBCSE pathway.

3

Indian Talent Olympiad (ITO)

The one a child can register for from home+
Classes1 to 10
SubjectsMaths, Science, English, Social Studies, Computer
Who registersSchool or the student directly — home registration is allowed, which is its real advantage
Forms openJuly–August, usually closing December–January
Exam monthsConducted in stages, so the window stays open longer
FeeAnnounced yearly on the official site
LevelsThree
PaperMCQ; can be attempted from home
Official siteindiantalent.org

Who should take it

  • Is in a school that does not run olympiads at all
  • Is a complete beginner and needs a gentle first exam
  • Wants practice more than a rank

What the child gets

Certificates, medals and cash awards for top ranks, with the exact amounts announced each year.

  • An easy first taste of a competitive paper
  • Useful when the school offers nothing
  • Builds the habit of sitting an exam outside school
Priyesh Sir’s note

Honestly: this is for practice, not for prestige. If your child's school runs SOF, do SOF first. ITO is the answer when the school runs nothing and you still want the child to start.

10

Unified Council — NSTSE, UIMO, UIEO, UCO

School-run practice papers with a national rank+
Classes1 to 10
ExamsNSTSE Science · UIMO Maths · UIEO English · UCO Computers
Who registersUsually the school; direct registration sometimes allowed
Forms openJuly–August, closing September–October
Exam monthsNovember to January, normally at the school itself
FeeAbout ₹150 to ₹200 per subject
LevelsUsually two; some exams are single level
PaperMCQ on OMR, 60–90 minutes, usually no negative marking
Official siteunifiedcouncil.com

Who should take it

  • Wants more MCQ practice at a moderate level
  • Needs to build speed and accuracy
  • Is working towards the bigger exams later

What the child gets

National rank certificates, gold, silver and bronze medals, cash awards for the top ranks and participation certificates.

  • Exam confidence and better speed
  • Stronger basics across subjects
  • A detailed performance report that shows where the child actually stands
Priyesh Sir’s note

NSTSE's feedback report is the useful part — it tells you which topics the child is weak in, which a school test never does. But be clear: this gives no government scholarship and no international pathway. It is practice with a good report card.

11

Spell Bee India

English spelling and vocabulary, Class 1 to 10+
Classes1 to 10
Conducted bySpellBee International
Who registersThe school, or the student directly online
Forms openJune–August, closing around September–October
Exam monthsLevel 1 in October–November · Level 2 in December–January · finals February to April
FeeAbout ₹150 to ₹300 per level
LevelsThree — school, state or zonal, then the national or international final
Official sitespellbeeinternational.com

Who should take it

  • Is weak in English and needs a reason to work on it
  • Enjoys words, reading and speaking
  • Needs confidence in front of an audience

What the child gets

Medals, certificates and cash awards for top ranks, with some editions going to an international level.

  • Vocabulary that helps in every other subject's paper
  • Clearer pronunciation and better communication
  • Confidence in public speaking
Priyesh Sir’s note

I do not teach English, so take this as a parent-to-parent suggestion rather than a teacher's. For a child who freezes in English, this exam does more good than another tuition class — it turns the language into a game instead of a subject.

Part two

The scholarship exams — where the real money is

Government exams, small fees or none at all, and support that can run for years. If your family qualifies for NMMS, it is worth more than every private olympiad on this page put together.

8

NMMS — National Means-cum-Merit Scholarship

₹12,000 a year from Class 9 to Class 12+
Class8 only — the scholarship then runs from Class 9 to Class 12
Conducted bythe Ministry of Education, through the State Education Departments
Who registersUsually the school; some states require the student to apply online
Forms openAugust–September, closing September–October
Exam monthsNovember–December at district or state centres
FeeNil to about ₹100 — several states charge nothing
EligibilityGovernment, aided or local body school · family income generally below ₹3.5 lakh · usually 55% in Class 7, with relaxations
LevelsOne state-level exam, with each state given a quota
PaperMAT and SAT (Science, Maths, Social Science), both MCQ
Official siteeducation.gov.in and your state council

Who should take it

  • Is in Class 8 in a government, aided or local body school
  • Comes from a family the income rule covers
  • Is academically sound and could use the support

What the child gets

₹12,000 a year — ₹1,000 a month — from Class 9 through Class 12, continuing as long as the child passes each year and keeps the required percentage.

  • Real money at the exact age when many families stop spending on education
  • Early exposure to a competitive paper
  • The same MAT and SAT skills that the state scholarship exam needs
Priyesh Sir’s note

If your child's school qualifies, do not skip this one. It is the single highest-value exam on this list for the effort involved, and it is easier than NTSE ever was. Check the income and school-type rules carefully — that is where most applications fail.

9

Maharashtra State Scholarship Exam

Class 5 and Class 8, run by MSCE Pune — the one every Maharashtra parent should know+
Classes5 and 8, for children studying in Maharashtra schools
Conducted byMaharashtra State Council of Examination, Pune
Who registersThe school, on the MSCE portal. Individual registration is uncommon
Forms openOctober–November, closing around December
Exam monthUsually February, offline
FeePaid through the school at registration
LevelsOne, with selection on merit, district quota and category
PaperPaper 1 mental ability · Paper 2 intelligence and subject knowledge (Maths, language, and general science for Class 8)
Official sitemscepune.in

Who should take it

  • Is in Class 5 or Class 8 in a Maharashtra school
  • Is quick at reasoning and enjoys puzzles
  • Should start building exam temperament early

What the child gets

A monthly scholarship, with the amount depending on class and category and revised by the state from time to time. Inside the school, the recognition matters as much as the money.

  • Financial help early
  • Reasoning practice that feeds straight into NMMS and NTSE-type papers
  • Confidence at an age when it is easy to build
Priyesh Sir’s note

This is the most popular exam in Maharashtra and the one I get asked about most in Mira Road and Bhayandar. Class 5 feels early, but the mental ability paper is a skill — the children who do well are simply the ones who practised it.

6

NTSE — National Talent Search Examination

Currently on hold, and you should know that before you plan for it+
StatusSuspended. NCERT stalled the scheme after the 2021-22 cycle and it has not restarted. Do not build a plan around it until NCERT announces a revamped scheme
Class10 only, when it runs
Conducted byNCERT, with Stage 1 run by each State Education Department
StagesTwo — state level, then national
PaperMAT (mental ability) and SAT (scholastic aptitude), both MCQ
Scholarship₹1,250 a month in Class 11 and 12, ₹2,000 a month at UG and PG, continuing to PhD under UGC norms
Official sitencert.nic.in

Who should take it

  • Nobody, for now — the exam is not being held
  • If it returns: a Class 10 student who is strong across subjects and good at reasoning

What the child gets

When it ran, qualifying both stages meant a scholarship that continued all the way to a PhD, plus the National Scholar certificate.

  • The MAT and SAT preparation is still useful — it overlaps with NMMS and the state scholarship exam
Priyesh Sir’s note

I have kept this chapter in the guide because parents still ask about it and coaching classes still sell it. The truth is that it has not been held since 2021-22. Prepare the same skills through NMMS and the Maharashtra scholarship exam instead, and if NTSE comes back your child is ready anyway.

Part three

The serious ones — for the child who enjoys hard problems

National-level exams with no cash prizes and a great deal of prestige. Do not start here. Come here when a child has already shown, over a year or two, that difficult problems make them curious rather than tired.

4

HBCSE Olympiads — IOQM and NSE

The real national pathway, run by the Government of India+
ClassesIOQM Maths, Class 8–12 · NSEJS Junior Science, Class 10 or below · NSEP, NSEC, NSEB for Class 11–12
Conducted byHBCSE (TIFR), with MTA for Maths and IAPT for the science papers
Who registersThe student, individually, online — not through the school
Forms closeGenerally end of September or early October
Exam monthsStage 1 in October–November · Stage 2 in January · camp in April–May
FeeAbout ₹200 to ₹300 — far less than the private olympiads
LevelsFour, ending at the International Olympiad
PaperDeeply conceptual, multi-step, negative marking in Stage 1
Official siteolympiads.hbcse.tifr.res.in · mtai.org.in · iapt.org.in

Who should take it

  • Is genuinely strong in Maths or Science, not just scoring well
  • Enjoys sitting with one hard problem for an hour
  • Is aiming at IIT, IISc or a research career

What the child gets

No cash prize. What you get instead is selection to the national training camp, the chance to represent India, international medals and certificates from the Government of India.

  • Recognition that IITs and IISc take seriously
  • Genuine problem-solving ability
  • It makes JEE Advanced feel easier, not harder
Priyesh Sir’s note

This is the serious one, and I will not pretend otherwise. It is much harder than SOF or SilverZone. If your child is not already enjoying hard problems, start with SOF for a year and come back to this. Registration is on the parent, not the school — that is where most children lose the chance.

7

NSEJS — National Standard Examination in Junior Science

The junior entry into the government olympiad pathway+
ClassesMainly 8, 9 and 10, subject to the age criteria in that year's notification
Conducted byIAPT, under HBCSE (TIFR)
Who registersThe student, online on the IAPT portal. Schools sometimes help but the responsibility is yours
Forms openAugust–September, closing around the end of September
Exam monthUsually November, offline at designated centres
FeeAbout ₹200 to ₹300
LevelsFour — NSEJS, INJSO, the OCSC camp, then IJSO
PaperMCQ, negative marking, two to three hours, Physics, Chemistry and Biology up to Class 10 depth
Official siteiapt.org.in

Who should take it

  • Is in Class 8 to 10 and genuinely enjoys science
  • Already has clear concepts, not just memorised answers
  • Is preparing seriously for JEE or NEET

What the child gets

No cash prize. Selection to the next stage, national recognition, and ultimately the chance to represent India at the International Junior Science Olympiad.

  • National scientific training exposure while still in school
  • A very strong foundation for JEE and NEET
  • Government recognition on the academic record
Priyesh Sir’s note

The syllabus stops at Class 10, but the questions go much deeper than Class 10. This is the exam I would point a strong Class 9 science student towards. Again — you register, not the school.

5

IITBMO — IIT Bombay Maths Olympiad

A single hard maths paper, written on the IIT Bombay campus+
ClassesGenerally 9 to 12; strong Class 8 students are sometimes allowed
Conducted bythe Mathematics Association, IIT Bombay
Who registersThe student, online. The school is not involved
Forms openAround December–January, closing late January or early February
Exam monthUsually February, offline at the IIT Bombay campus
FeeAround ₹399, paid during online registration
LevelsOne — a standalone competition, not a multi-stage ladder
TopicsAlgebra, geometry, number theory, logical reasoning, sometimes combinatorics
Official siteReleased each year on the IIT Bombay Mathematics Association page

Who should take it

  • Loves pure mathematics for its own sake
  • Is already preparing for IOQM or INMO
  • Wants a taste of IIT-level thinking before Class 11

What the child gets

Certificates of excellence and rank certificates from IIT Bombay. No large cash prize, but the certificate carries real academic weight.

  • A good bridge between school maths and olympiad maths
  • Confidence before attempting IOQM
  • The experience of writing a paper on an IIT campus
Priyesh Sir’s note

Being in Mumbai is an advantage here — the exam is held at Powai and most children in the country simply cannot travel for it. It does not give any admission advantage, so treat it as practice with a very good certificate attached.

12

CBSE Aryabhata Ganit Challenge

Free, CBSE-only, Class 8 to 10+
Classes8, 9 and 10, in CBSE-affiliated schools only
Conducted bythe Central Board of Secondary Education
Who registersThe school, on the CBSE portal. Students cannot apply on their own
NotificationUsually August–September, with registration closing in September
Exam monthsGenerally October–November, often online
FeeFree — it is a CBSE initiative
LevelsUsually two — school screening, then national
Official sitecbse.gov.in — look under Latest Announcements

Who should take it

  • Is in a CBSE school in Class 8 to 10
  • Likes maths and applies it rather than memorising it
  • Is already doing olympiads and wants one more

What the child gets

A CBSE certificate of merit and national-level recognition. No large cash prize.

  • It tests application of maths in daily life, not textbook sums
  • Recognition from the board the child already studies under
  • Good preparation for olympiad-style thinking
Priyesh Sir’s note

It costs nothing and it comes from your own board, so there is no reason to skip it — but the school has to register the child. Ask the maths teacher in August. State board and ICSE children are not eligible for this one.

Before you act on any of this

Every date, fee and prize on this page moves. Boards change the window by a month, organisers revise the fee, states rewrite their eligibility rules. I have written what is true as I publish this, and I have linked the official site for every single exam — check it before you pay anything.

One more thing, said plainly. Competitive exams are useful, and they are not a shortcut. A child who writes six olympiads and understands none of the chapters is worse off than a child who writes one and understands the subject. Pick one or two, prepare properly, and let the rest go. That is the whole strategy.

Questions parents ask

What parents ask me about these exams

My child is in Class 5. Which exam should we start with?
In Maharashtra, the state scholarship exam for Class 5 — the school handles the form, the fee is small, and the mental ability paper is a genuinely useful skill. Add SOF's IMO or NSO if the school runs them. Nothing else at that age.
Do these exams actually help in JEE or NEET later?
The olympiads do, because they train the child to read a hard question slowly instead of panicking. The scholarship exams help in a different way — they pay for the years when the tuition bills start. Neither gives any direct advantage in the JEE or NEET rank.
Is NTSE still held?
No. NCERT stalled the scheme after 2021-22 and it has not restarted. If a coaching class is selling you an NTSE batch right now, ask them to show you this year's notification.
Do you prepare students for these exams?
For the Maths and Science ones, yes — SOF, SilverZone, NSTSE, NMMS, the Maharashtra scholarship exam, NSEJS and IOQM. English and GK papers I do not teach, and I will say so rather than take the money and hand it to someone else.
