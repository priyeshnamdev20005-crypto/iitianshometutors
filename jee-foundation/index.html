The three-year plan

JEE & NEET Foundation, Class 8 to 10

The idea is simple and it is not new: do the work over four years instead of two. Basics in Class 8, the Class 11 syllabus begun in Class 9, the Class 12 syllabus opened in Class 10 — so Class 11 and 12 are spent on problems rather than on meeting everything for the first time.

The problem this solves

Class 11 is where good students break. Not because they are not capable, but because of the arithmetic of the year: in twelve months they must meet mechanics, the mole concept, organic chemistry and calculus for the first time, keep a school syllabus running, and start building the problem-solving speed that JEE and NEET actually test. Everything is new and everything is urgent, simultaneously.

Class 8, 9 and 10 have space that Class 11 does not. No board exam in Class 8 and 9, a light school syllabus, and a student who can afford to be confused for a week without losing a chapter. Using that space is the whole idea.

By Class 11, your child is looking at this content for the second time. Second time is a different experience.

The three years

What each one is for

Each year has its own page with the full plan, the subjects and the honest limits.

Year 1

Class 8 — the base

No JEE questions. The year is spent making algebra automatic, units instinctive and chemical notation routine — the three things that quietly decide how fast everything afterwards can go.

Olympiad questions used to keep the subject interesting rather than to chase a medal.

Year 2

Class 9 — Class 11 begins

School board work first, and alongside it the Class 11 syllabus properly begun: mechanics from the derivation, the mole concept until it is boring, trigonometry early because physics needs it.

Four to six hours a week. No board exam in the way.

Year 3

Class 10 — Class 12 opened

A board year, so the board comes first and January and February are board-only. Around it, calculus, organic chemistry and electrostatics get opened, and the April–May gap after the boards is used properly.

An honest word about promisesNo teacher can promise your child a rank or a selection, and any teacher who does is selling you something. What an early start genuinely buys is time — the syllabus met twice instead of once, and Class 11 and 12 spent on problems rather than on first exposure. That is a real advantage and it is the one I will put in writing.

Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a student who is comfortable in their school syllabus, who finds the subject interesting enough to ask why, and who has four to six hours a week to give. Board does not matter — CBSE, ICSE, SSC, IGCSE and MYP students all run this plan, with small adjustments.

It does not suit a student who is currently struggling with the school syllabus itself. In that case the school work is the job, and adding a second syllabus on top produces pressure without progress. If that is your child, I will say so on the first call, teach the board syllabus properly, and we can revisit this in a year.

It also does not suit a family looking for a guarantee. Nobody can promise a rank or a selection — those depend on four years of work, on aptitude, and on a national exam with over a million candidates. What an early start buys is time, and time is worth a great deal. It is just not the same thing as a promise.

What comes after

Questions parents ask

Foundation classes — what parents ask me

Which class should we start in?

Class 8 gives the most room, Class 9 is the most common starting point, and Class 10 still works with a shorter plan. The honest answer depends on how your child is doing in school right now, so call me and tell me. If the school syllabus is a struggle, the right answer is board tuition first and foundation work later — and I will tell you that rather than sell you the bigger package.

Is this JEE coaching or school tuition?

Both, in that order. The school board syllabus is taught and comes first every single week; the foundation work runs alongside it. A student who ends Class 10 with a weak board result and a good foundation notebook has not gained anything, and I am not interested in producing that.

Can you really complete the JEE syllabus in Class 9 and 10?

No, and I would not believe anyone who says they can. What is realistic is meeting most of the Class 11 syllabus and opening the Class 12 syllabus across those two years, so that Class 11 and 12 become a second pass instead of a first one. That is a real, substantial advantage — and it is a different claim from finishing the syllabus.

Do you guarantee a rank or selection?

No. No teacher can, and the ones who advertise it are counting on you not asking what happens when it does not come true. Selection depends on your child's work over four years, their aptitude, and a national exam with lakhs of candidates. What I commit to is the plan, the material, a marked test every week, an honest update every week, and telling you plainly if it is not working.

Do you teach Biology?

No. Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics only. I would rather tell you that on the first call than take the subject, teach it out of a guide book and waste your child's year. For Biology you keep a separate teacher, and plenty of my students do exactly that.

What are your fees?

Fees depend on the class, how many subjects you want and how many classes a week we run. All the material — notes, worksheets, question banks and the weekly marked test — is included, so the number I quote is the number you pay. Call or WhatsApp on 79774 75848 with your child's class, board and subjects and I will give you the exact figure.

Is the demo class really free?

Yes, and it is a real class on a real chapter — not a sales meeting. Tell me what your child is stuck on and we will do exactly that. At the end you will know whether my teaching suits your child, which is the only question worth answering.
